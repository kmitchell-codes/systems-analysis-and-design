Reflection: 

1. Briefly summarize the DriverPass project. Who was the client? What type of system did they want you to design?

The client was DriverPass, a company that wanted a system to help students prepare for DMV driving tests. The system needed the system to have online practice tests, ability to schedule online, payment ability, and the ability to schedule optional on-the-road training and connection to the DMV in order for real-time updates.

2. What did you do particularly well?

I did well at organizing the client’s requirements and making sure that the design matched their needs. I also placed some focus on security and cloud setup so that the system would be safe and scalable.

3. If you could choose one part of your work on these documents to revise, what would you pick? How would you improve it?

If I could choose one part of my work to revise, I’d revise the diagrams. They’re functional and clear but could use clearer labels and more detail to make the workflows easier to follow.

4. How did you interpret the user’s needs and implement them into your system design? Why is it so important to consider the user’s needs when designing?

It’s important to design with the user in mind because even the best tech doesn’t matter if people can’t use it easily. The end user is the one that will be using the system day in and day out and determines the success of the system. I looked at what students, instructors, and admins would actually do in the system in order to determine what the needs were for each user.

5. How do you approach designing software? What techniques or strategies would you use in the future to analyze and design a system?

I start by gathering requirements, split them into functional and nonfunctional, then use diagrams to map it all out. In the future, I’d add more user feedback early on to catch issues before the design is finalized.
